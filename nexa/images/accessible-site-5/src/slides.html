can you read this?
Why it hurts: older eyes, low vision, or just a phone outside in the Aussie sun. If people can't read your hours or prices, they don't squint. They leave.
Fix: dark text on a light background. Search "WebAIM contrast checker" and aim for at least 4.5 to 1 on normal text.

4 links, 1 thumb
Why it hurts: shaky hands, sore joints, big thumbs or a bumpy bus. People miss, hit the wrong link, try again, then give up.
Fix: make your main buttons big and easy to hit, about the size of a fingertip, with space between them. Booking and calling come first.

SCREEN READER: LINKS
1. click here
2. click here
3. click here
4. here
...which one?
Why it hurts: screen readers read a page out loud, and their users often jump through a list of just the links. Four "click here"s tell them nothing. Google also reads link words to work out what the page is about.
Fix: let the link say where it goes. "See our fees". "Book an appointment".

can't tap to call
Why it hurts: screen readers can't read words baked into an image. Zoom in and it goes blurry. Nobody can tap the number or copy the address. And Google reads real text far better than pictures.
Fix: type your hours, prices and phone number as real text on the page. Keep the pretty graphic for socials.

what's wrong with it?
Why it hurts: grey hint text inside the box disappears as soon as you type, so people lose track of what each box was for. And if the only sign of a mistake is a red border, colour blind visitors may never see it. Form fails, enquiry gone.
Fix: a label above every box, and errors in plain words. "Please add your full phone number."
